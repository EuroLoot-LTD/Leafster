## Draft community rules

Leafster centres on videos and constructive public conversation. These rules apply to uploads, profiles, titles, thumbnails, Leaves, Stories, comments and Community Notes.

- No child sexual exploitation, grooming, coercion, sexual solicitation of minors or attempts to obtain exploitative material. Do not encourage secret contact or use outside services to evade safety restrictions.
- No threats, targeted harassment, hateful abuse, stalking, doxxing or sharing private contact details without a lawful basis. Do not impersonate, scam, spam or manipulate reports, notes or engagement.
- No sexual exploitation, nonconsensual intimate material or other illegal content. [Define any broader prohibited adult/graphic categories consistently with the age/safety design before launch].

- Add sources and relevant context to Community Notes. Do not include personal information, harassment or unsupported accusations. Helpful votes are one input into review, not a determination of truth.
- Use block/report controls and the monitored safety route. Report urgent credible threats promptly through the appropriate authority route; do not redistribute suspected illegal material or investigate vulnerable users yourself.

## Draft illegal-content and copyright notice procedure

**This procedure cannot go live until its monitored submission route and review team exist.** The route must be usable by people who are not account holders, accessible and easy to find. In-product reports supplement it.

1. **Submit a notice.** Identify the exact Leafster URL/content identifier, explain why the material is allegedly illegal and provide supporting information. For copyright, identify the protected work, relevant rights/authority and the allegedly infringing item. Provide a reply contact and good-faith accuracy/authority statement, subject to lawful exceptions. Never require unnecessary identity documents or publication of a reporter's identity. An EU procedure should not automatically impose US DMCA declarations, jurisdiction or deadlines.
2. **Acknowledge and triage.** Issue a reference, keep receipt time, restrict access to the report and prioritise credible safety threats. Seek clarification where essential. Review promptly and impartially; do not assume that a high report count establishes illegality.
3. **Decide and notify.** Preserve only necessary evidence securely, record the actual legal/rule ground and decision, act expeditiously where required, and explain outcomes and redress to the appropriate parties without exposing confidential information. Trained staff handle authority escalation where required. [DSA notice/action and reasons](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065).
4. **Challenge or counter-notice.** Permit the affected uploader to explain ownership, permission, misidentification or a lawful exception and submit relevant evidence. Arrange qualified human reconsideration; restore unjustifiably restricted material when appropriate. Do not automatically restore after an invented fixed countdown. [Irish copyright Regulations](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2021/si/567/made/en/print).
5. **Close and retain proportionately.** Keep an access-controlled decision/appeal record for the approved period, honour valid legal holds and delete unnecessary material. Track errors and repeat abuse, including abusive reporting. Publish any required aggregate transparency information without private data.

A copyright notice process is only one part of the rights-clearance obligations that may apply to a content-sharing service. The operator must obtain professional advice on classification, licences, exceptions, proportionality and any required prevention measures before public launch.
